# Question 6 — AI Literacy: Prompt Engineering

**Format:** Short Answer

---

Read both prompts below carefully, then answer the questions that follow.

**Prompt A**

> I am a marketing manager at a retail company and we have just finished a three-month campaign. My team has collected customer feedback through an online survey and we now have about 500 responses stored in a spreadsheet. Each response includes the customer's age group, the product they purchased, their satisfaction rating from 1 to 5, and a short written comment. I need to present the findings to our CEO next Friday in a way that is easy to understand. Can you analyse this data for me, highlight which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores, identify the most common complaints from the written comments, and summarise everything in a short paragraph I can use as an executive summary?

**Prompt B**

> **Role:** You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.  
> **Task:** Analyse customer survey data from a 3-month campaign.  
> **Data:** 500 responses containing age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating (1-5), and written comments.  
> **Steps:**
> 1. Identify age groups and products with the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in an executive summary paragraph.
>
> **Audience:** CEO presentation on Friday.  
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise and free of technical jargon.

---

## Task 1 — Analysis

**(a)** Which prompt do you think will get a better response from an AI? State your choice (A or B).

**Answer:**

Choice B 
---

**(b)** Give **two reasons** to support your choice.

**Reason 1:**  
Prompt B breaks the task into explicit, numbered steps (identify lowest scores → extract themes → summarize), which gives the AI a clear sequence to follow rather than making it infer the right order and scope from one long run-on sentence. Structured steps reduce the chance the AI skips or under-weights part of the task.

**Reason 2:**  
Prompt B explicitly separates role, audience, and constraints ("data analyst," "CEO presentation," "concise and free of technical jargon"). Prompt A buries the audience and tone requirements inside narrative context, making them easy for the model to under-prioritize. Naming constraints explicitly and separately makes them much more likely to actually shape the output.

---

**(c)** What is **one strength** of the prompt you did NOT choose?

**Answer:**  
Prompt A reads more naturally and includes real narrative context (why this matters, the business situation) that can help the AI understand intent, not just mechanics — sometimes useful for nuance or tone-matching. It's more like how a person would actually explain a task to a colleague.
---

## Task 2 — Rewrite

Rewrite either prompt by borrowing **one element** from the other to make it stronger. Then explain what you borrowed and why.

**Your rewritten prompt:**

>**Role:**: You are a data analyst helping a retail marketing team.
>**Context:**: We just wrapped a three-month retail campaign and collected ~500 customer survey responses (age group, product purchased, satisfaction rating 1–5, and a written comment) to understand how it landed with customers.
>**Task:**: Analyse this data and prepare findings for a CEO presentation this Friday.
> **Steps:**

> 1. Identify which age groups and products have the lowest satisfaction scores.
> 2. Extract the most common complaint themes from the written comments.
> 3. Summarise findings in a short executive summary paragraph.
> **Audience:** assume no technical background.
> **Constraints:** Keep the summary concise, non-technical, and business-focused.

**What you borrowed and why:**  
I borrowed Prompt A's real-world context (why this data exists, what campaign it's from) and added it as a short "Context" section within Prompt B's structured format. This keeps B's clarity and explicit steps while giving the AI enough situational grounding to produce a response that feels tailored to this specific campaign rather than a generic satisfaction-survey summary.
